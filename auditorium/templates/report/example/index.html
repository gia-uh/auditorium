District water office, March 2027

Water use in the district's schools

Where the water goes, and the three repairs that save the most

Summary

The district's 24 schools used 61,000 cubic metres of water in 2026, a fifth more than in 2024. Most of the rise comes from leaks in old buildings, not from more pupils: the six schools built before 1970 use twice as much water per pupil as the rest.

Fixing leaking cisterns, replacing the oldest taps and metering each building would cut use by about a quarter. The repairs cost less than one year of the water they save.

How we measured

Each school reads its main meter on the first day of every month. We compared those readings with the number of pupils enrolled and the days the school was open, so a long holiday does not look like a saving.

In the six oldest schools we also read the meter at night, when nobody uses water. Any flow at night is a leak, and every one of the six had some.

What comes next

The office will repair the cisterns of the six oldest schools before the summer break, when the buildings are empty. Taps follow in the autumn, and every building gets its own meter by the end of the year.

We will publish the readings each month, so every school can see its own use next to the district's.
